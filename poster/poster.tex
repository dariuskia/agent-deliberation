        % --- LEFT COLUMN ---
        \sectionheading{Motivation}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.4cm}
          \item Democratic deliberation requires every voice to be heard, but human-scale forums break down beyond small groups. Can AI agents deliberate \textit{on behalf of} citizens while faithfully representing their values?
          \item Citizens hold both \textbf{public values} they openly express and \textbf{private values} they never voice --- fears, doubts, and contradictions that shape their true satisfaction with outcomes.
          \item Centralized approaches (a single AI mediator synthesizing all input) are simple but risk compressing minority perspectives. Fully decentralized approaches (one AI delegate per citizen) preserve representation but scale poorly.
          \item We ask: \textit{Which deliberation architecture best balances overall consensus quality with minority satisfaction as the number of participants grows?}
        \end{itemize}

        \sectionheading{Methods}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.4cm}
          \item We simulate deliberation among 50 diverse AI-generated American citizen profiles, each with detailed backgrounds, public values, and hidden private values.
          \item Three architectures on a centralized--decentralized spectrum:
            \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2cm}
              \item[\textcolor{pivotalOrange}{$\blacksquare$}] \textbf{Centralized:} A single AI mediator drafts consensus; citizens provide feedback over 3 rounds.
              \item[\textcolor{pivotalBlue}{$\blacklozenge$}] \textbf{Clustered:} An LLM groups citizens by position similarity, synthesizes AI ``candidates'' per cluster, candidates deliberate among themselves, then citizens vote via instant-runoff.
              \item[\textcolor{pivotalGray}{$\bullet$}] \textbf{Delegates:} Each citizen has a dedicated AI delegate that deliberates on their behalf over 3 rounds, proposes consensus, then citizens vote.
            \end{itemize}
          \item Scaling experiment: $N \in \{4, 10, 50\}$ participants across 3 polarizing policy questions (AI in courts, minimum wage, immigration).
          \item Satisfaction scored 1--10 by each citizen's AI persona against \textit{both} public and private values. Key metrics: average satisfaction, bottom-quartile (minority) satisfaction, variance.
        \end{itemize}

        \sectionheading{Takeaways}
        \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.4cm}
          \item \textbf{Centralized mediation degrades at scale}: a single mediator cannot compress 50 diverse perspectives without losing minority voices, leading to high variance and low worst-case satisfaction.
          \item \textbf{Delegates preserve minority satisfaction}: dedicated per-citizen representation maintains bottom-quartile scores even at $N=50$, at the cost of higher computational overhead.
          \item \textbf{Clustered deliberation is the scalable middle ground}: reducing $N$ citizens to $K$ AI candidates captures coalition structure while keeping deliberation tractable, with Pareto improvements on the satisfaction--equity frontier.
          \item \textbf{Hidden values matter}: satisfaction measured against only public values overestimates consensus quality; private fears and contradictions reveal deeper dissatisfaction that surface-level agreement masks.
          \item Future work: human-in-the-loop validation, adaptive cluster sizing, and multi-round citizen feedback to AI delegates.
        \end{itemize}
